# From Riverbend to an Empire
## Approved game direction · 20 November 2026

**North Star: one capital you grow through the ages, and a network of places you build for a reason and revisit because you want to.**

This changes what the player builds, not just what the game is called. Riverbend is still a Stone Age settlement at the start. It earns its role as capital when the player needs something beyond its borders. A mining village, forest town or island harbor can remain small forever, become more useful, or grow into a city by choice. None is a second capital the player has to manage every day. “Empire” is the design direction, not a final title or a request to rename the repo or app.

## Starting capital, ruler and empire identity

- **Riverbend is the fixed starting settlement in the main campaign.** It becomes the capital as the empire forms. The first geography choice is where to found a Bronze Age support settlement, not where to start New Game. Alternate starting capitals are a possible later campaign option, not part of the first playable slice.
- **New Game names the ruler.** Ask “What are you called?” with a prefilled suggestion from a curated name bank (e.g. “Sulla au Augustus”), **Reroll**, and free-text edit. Let players choose **Male, Female, or Neutral**. Give each a distinct default character portrait, and let players pick any portrait regardless of gender. For Neutral, use a fully realized founder, not a silhouette or blend: the proposed Riverbend portrait has cropped curls, a blue woven cloak, and a carved river-stone clasp, with the same visual care and warm campfire lighting as the other two. This is a portrait brief for later coordination, not an instruction to interrupt the visual branch.
- **Titles and names are separate.** Start with **Founder** for all three. At Bronze, suggest **King**, **Queen**, or **Sovereign** respectively; players can change or omit the title. Keep the ruler’s name and character. As the world map opens, ask “What shall your empire be called?” and save that answer separately. Riverbend remains the city name. The empire name is not needed in the Stone Age.
- **New Game stays short.** Accept the suggested name and portrait quickly; reroll only on request. Store ruler and, later, empire identity per campaign. A legacy save with no identity data must continue to open without a forced prompt, reset, or overwritten progress. Plan any fuller legacy adoption separately.

## The two progression loops

- **Capital across time.** Keep Riverbend on the same landscape. Improve useful buildings in place, repurpose or replace others, and let players keep selected landmarks where they were built. A Stone Age shrine could eventually become an archaeological site. Preserve the civic center’s identity. One empire-wide era and research record determines what is known; each settlement chooses what it actually builds. No blanket auto-upgrades or compulsory tours after every age.
- **Empire across geography.** A material Riverbend lacks gives the player a reason to explore the world. Several sites can solve the same need, but each offers a different extra resource, terrain challenge, and technology or tool opportunity. Once established, settlements cover their own essentials and ship surplus automatically. Visiting is for discoveries, creative building and optional projects, not keeping the empire alive.

## Milestones, in dependency order

### E0 · Finish Riverbend as the origin story
**Goal:** Make the Stone Age a satisfying first session before adding another map.

Use the existing onboarding, economy and Map Finds plans. The first hut leads to discovering a forest, establishing production and learning the basics by doing. While a real task is running, the player can explore or follow an optional find. The first era ends with a clear sense that Riverbend grew from a camp into a settlement. Do not add an empire menu, an expansion requirement or a new first-era economy in parallel.

**Gate:** A fresh player can accept or edit a suggested founder name, choose any of the three gender options and any portrait without a cross-restriction, and retain that identity after reload. They can then reach the existing Bronze readiness milestone, recover from a resource shortage and skip every map find. Use human playthrough feedback as well as automated checks. Codex’s visual work continues independently.

### E1 · Play the Bronze transition on the same map
**Goal:** Prove that stepping into a new age feels like evolving Riverbend, not starting over.

Make the Bronze transition playable in a review scenario. Prove one productive building evolution, one preserved landmark and one Bronze project that needs an imported material. Enter Bronze before requiring an outside settlement. Test a representative mature capital layout with roads, older buildings, a preserved site and room for one new district. If it does not fit, try unlocking contiguous edge land without moving existing coordinates before committing to a larger map.

**Gate:** The old town is recognizable and usable. The player knows what they lack and why going outside Riverbend helps. At world-map unlock, the empire name and chosen title persist separately from the unchanged city name and ruler identity. No demolition or map reset is required just to advance.

### E2 · Open the world map and found the first support settlement
**Goal:** Make the first regional choice meaningful and get one dependable automatic shipment back to Riverbend.

Prototype two sites that provide the same required material. Illustrative pair: a rocky upland with tin and building stone, and a coastal island with tin and salt. Show the needed resource, bonus resource, terrain constraint and likely unlock before commitment. Building stone might unlock retaining walls; salt might unlock food preservation. These are test candidates, not fixed catalog entries. Both routes must solve the core need without requiring the player to pick the stronger long-term reward. Reuse empire knowledge so a new village skips the original tutorial grind, but still needs local labor, materials and facilities.

After a short local setup, the settlement can enter stewardship: essential reserve first, established route quotas next, optional routes last. Show what is shipped, what stays home and why a shipment is paused. A full destination or a tight reserve pauses the transfer without losing stock. Returning later or relaunching must not duplicate it. No need to open the village to collect. Add strategic remote actions, such as upgrading a port from the empire menu with a confirmed cost and outcome, once the basic route works.

**Gate:** Separate playthroughs prove both site choices can supply the capital. The settlement remains stable and ships again when conditions allow, even when never visited after setup. Swapping maps or restarting does not reset either place.

### E3 · Make it worth going back
**Goal:** While Riverbend is building, researching or gathering, give a player an enjoyable few minutes somewhere else.

Prototype one repeatable visit loop in one support settlement before producing a full regional catalog:
- **Notice:** small, terrain-specific discoveries, such as deer tracks, a washed-up crate or a tide pool. Small local rewards are fine; no streaks, expiring chores or rare materials needed for the main path.
- **Make it yours:** decorations, planting, paths, rearranging and optional building projects. Some changes are cosmetic; others open a new piece of terrain or give a modest permanent local benefit. Example: a restored footbridge opens a cove; a repaired smokehouse improves local food preservation. A larger restoration can offer a real choice, such as a fishing dock or ferry landing, with consequences shown upfront.
- **Change pace:** quieter soundscapes, such as gulls and water on the island or wind and wildlife inland, with music optional. Let the player stay after the capital finishes; notify without pulling them away.

**Gate:** In a short playtest, see whether people choose to visit, discover or change something, and sometimes stay after the capital is ready. Changes survive reload. A player who skips all visits can still progress normally. Do not solve idle time by making capital timers longer.

### E4 · Let settlements build things together
**Goal:** Prove one optional settlement-to-settlement route and one shared side project.

Keep the island’s tin shipments to Riverbend intact. Separately, a forest village swaps spare timber for island salt. The forest uses the salt for a smokehouse project; the island uses the timber to restore or choose a use for its pier. Let the player visit both ends and see the result. The route is set once and then runs automatically using actual spare stock, never the origin’s essentials or resources reserved for existing routes. If either side lacks its contribution or capacity, show the reason and wait without partial loss. No capital relay, manual loading, or daily collection.

**Recommendation for the first pilot:** Treat it as a finite contract that completes and frees the route up. Test automatic recurring exchange later only if it adds value without another obligation. This lifecycle is a proposal, not a locked decision.

**Gate:** With the route enabled, players can finish the side project and see durable changes in both places. With it disabled, capital supply and progress still work. Prove that low stock, full storage, and save/reload do not consume or duplicate either leg of the exchange.

### E5 · Add single-player threats without adding chores
**Goal:** Once basic trade is dependable, test one regional disruption that gives the player a decision without making visits mandatory.

Start with a bounded coastal storm that delays one shipment. Show the cause, expected recovery and available choices: wait for automatic resumption, reroute at a visible cost or longer travel time, or invest in a sheltered harbor against similar future disruption. Do not destroy cargo, buildings or reserves while the player is away. The default wait must resolve on its own and must not permanently block era progress. Don’t stack disruptions or ship a combat system to test this loop. Keep the exact frequency and duration open until playtest.

**Gate:** Ignoring the storm preserves cargo and essentials and automatically resumes supply. A player who engages has a meaningful choice, not a compulsory repair bill. Keep NPC bandits or pirates as optional future encounters only if the noncombat game works; no PvP or empire-versus-empire warfare.

### E6 · Scale from a pair of settlements to an empire
**Goal:** Make additional ages and terrain deepen the choices without increasing the daily workload.

Rehearse an empire with Riverbend and three stable settlements, not three mandatory check-ins. Test advanced material chains, such as a later iron supplier supporting steel, along with local specialization, remote strategic upgrades and age-appropriate architectural and audio variants. Grow the regional catalog only where terrain changes how the player builds or explores. Regions inspired by the Mediterranean, Central Asia, China and Japan are later candidates, not a promised launch list. Consider optional regional research branches without fracturing the shared knowledge record or locking out the basic path.

**Gate:** In playtest, the player can focus on the capital or one chosen project. Old supply routes remain legible, future villages need not grow into cities, and earlier landmarks still tell the story of the capital. Expansion adds choices, not maintenance rounds.

## First playable proof of the new direction

After E0, build a small reviewed slice across E1–E3: **Enter Bronze → see two credible places to source a missing material → found one village → get an unattended shipment → return for one optional improvement that remains on the map.** Use placeholder regional assets; no need to wait for a full regional art set. Prototype village-to-village projects and then single-player disruptions only once this loop works. If choosing a region feels cosmetic, or people visit only to fix shortages, tune this slice before adding more ages, regions or content.

## Codex handoff and guardrails

- This roadmap records approved direction, not authorization to implement all milestones. Use this plan alongside the canonical roadmap. Reconcile existing IDs in each bounded spec rather than silently rewriting older decisions. Turn only the next separately approved milestone into a bounded Codex Goal. Include specific acceptance checks and use the existing `scripts/dev.py` QA workflow after reading its current documentation. Report what was actually tested and stop for review.
- Keep existing normal/developer saves intact. Start risky multi-settlement and accounting work in an isolated review scenario. Production adoption or save migration needs its own review. Keep existing art, first-era QA and unrelated branches untouched.
- No multiplayer, paid materials, rewarded timers, PvP, empire-versus-empire warfare, conquest, new backend or full rebrand in this roadmap. Keep ordinary progress independent of ads or purchases; later monetization is a separate design review.
- Explicit decisions before a shipping spec: final Bronze materials and costs, whether a slower outside trade fallback exists without founding a settlement, offline catch-up limits, route priorities/quotas and project repeatability. Examples here are not approved tuning or a final era list.
