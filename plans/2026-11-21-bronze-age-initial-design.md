# Bronze Age: first regional expansion

**Status: initial design draft, not approved for implementation.** Revisit only after Stone Age onboarding, menus, and the full first era have been played and accepted by Dima, and he explicitly gives the go-ahead for Bronze. No code, queue, or schedule follows from this draft.

## What the player sees

1. **A reason to look outward.** Continue the same Riverbend save. The first Bronze project needs tin. Riverbend can provision an expedition, but there is no local tin. Local copper is a working assumption, not a balance decision.
2. **A bigger map, taught by the same guide.** The contextual guide used in Stone Age points to the World Map. Riverbend is known. Fog leaves enough visible to recognize broad regions such as a coastline or forest, not exact hills, shoreline, tiles or buildable space. Do not show exact deposits before scouting.
3. **Send scouts, then return to Riverbend.** Let the player keep building or doing optional activities while scouts are away. On return, compare two reports: tin, bonus resource, land or sea route, founding supplies, and the main local constraint. Make the choices easy to compare, not a series of blind trials.
4. **Choose and found one support settlement.** Reveal the site’s actual playable terrain on arrival. Once the second settlement exists, Riverbend becomes the capital and the guide invites the player to name the empire, with a suggested name they can keep or change. The founder’s personal name is separate.
5. **Prove that the network works.** Set a basic route, then show the first automatic tin shipment arriving at Riverbend. A stable support settlement supplies the network without daily maintenance. Visiting, decorating, and improving the route are choices. Use the same contextual guide component, not a separate Bronze tutorial system.

## Two sites for the first design

| Site | What it offers | First challenge | Viable bootstrap using Riverbend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Island | Tin + salt via sea | Little timber or farmland; needs a landing and basic sea route | Send ordinary Wood and Food for the landing and camp. A basic pier is sufficient for first tin; hardwood is an upgrade, not a prerequisite. |
| Forest | Tin + hardwood via land | Tin mine is inland, so hauling is slow | Send ordinary Wood and Food for a basic road/cart route. The first haul works without salt or a second settlement. |

Riverbend’s ordinary Wood remains useful and available at home. The island’s local timber shortage creates a supply decision, not a shutdown of the capital. Hardwood is a distinct optional improvement, not a replacement for ordinary Wood. Before founding, disclose the minimum supplies and basic route so the player can plan without needing the other site.

## Salt and Food Preservation

Expeditions spend Food because the party eats its provisions on the journey. Discovering Salt exposes **Food Preservation** as a research unlock. Completing that research unlocks higher food storage capacity and longer-range expeditions, so Salt opens new places to explore rather than simply adding another stockpile.

The first scout trips and both basic tin routes must remain possible with ordinary Food and Wood. Preservation extends the range beyond those first choices; it is not needed to reach the island that supplies Salt. Exact storage amounts, ration costs, and range thresholds are still open. This does not commit the game to a general food spoilage system.

## How founding order changes the game

- **Island first → Forest later:** Start with a basic pier and sea deliveries. Forest hardwood could later upgrade the island pier, carry more cargo, or supply repairs. A direct Forest–Island supply route is a possible later improvement, not a requirement for the first shipment.
- **Forest first → Island later:** Start with a basic inland haul. Discovering Island salt lets the player research Food Preservation, unlock more food storage, and send longer expeditions. The first land route works without it.
- **Third site later, if useful:** A mountain pass might offer tin and building stone, with steep hauling and little farmland. Stone could shelter the island harbor or harden the forest road; hardwood could improve mountain carts, and salt could extend its food reserves. Keep the mountain optional and revisit site count once the two-route loop is fun.

## What to test before locking prices or scope

- Either initial site, on its own, can get the first automatic tin shipment home using reachable Riverbend resources. No route, input, or upgrade depends on establishing the other site.
- The scout reports explain why both choices work and what each struggles with, without spoiling the actual local map.
- The player can choose the other site later, in either order, and the optional improvements feel meaningfully different. Do not force both settlements just to complete the first Bronze objective.
- A player can distinguish “I need tin to progress” from “this upgrade would improve my route.” There is something useful to do in Riverbend while waiting.
- Revisit scout timing and costs, route throughput, founding costs and whether it pays to add a third choice. Exact recipes, timing, prices, and unlocks are not decided here.
